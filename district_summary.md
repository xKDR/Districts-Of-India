Below I treat “2016–21” as the period 2016 through FY2020-21. One caveat: India did not conduct the planned 2021 Census, so population figures for this period are generally 2011 Census baselines or administrative estimates, not comparable 2021 census counts.

1. Mewat / Nuh, Haryana

Generally: Mewat—officially Nuh district since 2016—lies south of Delhi along the Aravalli hills and is predominantly Meo. It has a hot, semi-arid climate, with very low and variable rainfall. Historically, Mewat was a strategic frontier around Delhi and was associated with the Meo community and medieval Rajput/Islamic polities.

Economically, 2016–21: Agriculture was overwhelmingly dominant, mostly rain-fed, supplemented by dairy, goats/sheep and local retail. The district remained one of Haryana's poorer regions, with weak industrialization despite proximity to NCR; Rojka-Meo industrial estate and MMTC-PAMP were notable formal industry. Development efforts focused heavily on roads, irrigation, education and basic services.

2. Pithoragarh, Uttarakhand

Generally: Pithoragarh is Uttarakhand's easternmost Himalayan district, bordering Nepal and Tibet/China. Elevation ranges from subtropical valleys to permanently snow-covered alpine areas, producing highly varied climates. Historically it was part of the Kumaon region and has long been important for Himalayan trade, pilgrimage and border administration.

Economically, 2016–21: The economy remained largely rural, based on agriculture, horticulture, livestock, government employment, small trade and tourism. Border geography gives it strategic importance and creates demand for roads and defence-related infrastructure, while out-migration of working-age residents remained a structural constraint. Tourism and pilgrimage offered growing but geographically uneven opportunities.

3. West Tripura

Generally: West Tripura occupies the western part of Tripura along the Bangladesh border, with Agartala as its capital and administrative centre. It has a humid subtropical/monsoonal climate, dense vegetation and relatively low-lying terrain. The area is central to Tripura's modern political history and to the state's transformation following Partition and independence.

Economically, 2016–21: This was the state's most urbanized/commercially important district, with Agartala driving government services, retail, construction, transport and small-scale industry. Agriculture, rubber, bamboo/forest products and food processing remained important outside the capital; the Bangladesh border also offered substantial trade/logistics potential. COVID-19 in 2020–21 disrupted commerce and small businesses, with state relief measures documented in the 2021-22 economic review.

4. Gonda, Uttar Pradesh

Generally: Gonda is in eastern Uttar Pradesh's fertile Gangetic plain, centred on Gonda town and crossed by the Ghaghra/Saryu river system. It has a hot subtropical monsoon climate and extensive agricultural land. Its history is tied to the wider Awadh/Devipatan region, with numerous Hindu religious and cultural sites.

Economically, 2016–21: Agriculture dominated—especially rice, wheat, maize and sugarcane—with sugar mills forming the principal industrial base; pottery and yarn-related activities were smaller industries. With roughly 3.4 million people in 2011, the district was overwhelmingly rural, making agricultural productivity, irrigation and rural employment particularly important. COVID-19 and associated containment measures were a major 2020 economic disruption.

5. South Garo Hills, Meghalaya

Generally: South Garo Hills is a heavily forested, mountainous district in southern Meghalaya bordering Bangladesh, headquartered at Baghmara. It was created in 1992 and has a very wet, warm-humid monsoon climate. The landscape, Garo culture and Balpakram/Siju areas give it considerable ecological and tourism significance.

Economically, 2016–21: Agriculture and horticulture were the core livelihood base—rice, areca nut, pineapple, banana, spices, rubber and other cash crops—alongside livestock, weaving and bamboo/cane crafts. Mining was also significant historically, particularly coal and limestone, although environmental/regulatory restrictions sharply constrained coal mining. Poor roads and market access limited commercialization; improving connectivity and farmer-market linkages became important development priorities.

6. Sivasagar, Assam

Generally: Sivasagar is in Upper Assam and was the historic capital of the Ahom kingdom, which ruled much of Assam for over six centuries. It is flat, extremely rainy and humid, with extensive wetlands and flood-prone areas. Its Ahom monuments—Sivasagar tank, Sivadol, Rang Ghar and Talatal Ghar—make it one of Assam's major heritage areas.

Economically, 2016–21: Sivasagar had an unusually diversified rural economy: tea + oil & natural gas + agriculture. ONGC's Assam Asset is headquartered at Nazira and operates fields including Geleki, Lakwa and Rudrasagar; tea estates are also major employers. Flooding remained a recurring economic risk, while the oil/tea sectors provided a much stronger industrial base than in many northeastern districts.

7. Kargil, Ladakh

Generally: Kargil is a high-altitude, extremely cold and arid Himalayan district between Srinagar and Leh, extending toward Zanskar. It historically served as a Central Asian/Himalayan trading crossroads. The 1999 Kargil War fundamentally shaped its modern identity, while its Shia Muslim population and Buddhist heritage give it a distinctive cultural character.

Economically, 2016–21: Agriculture remained crucial despite the harsh climate—over 90% of residents were rural, with small holdings and a short April–September/October growing season. Tourism was an important growth sector: arrivals rose from about 64,000 in 2016 to 102,000 in 2017, before COVID devastated tourism in 2020–21. Defence/public spending, horticulture, handicrafts and cross-regional trade were other important sources of income.

8. Kinnaur, Himachal Pradesh

Generally: Kinnaur is a high Himalayan border district adjoining Tibet, centred around Reckong Peo, with the Sutlej, Baspa and Spiti valleys. It ranges from relatively mild valleys to cold alpine terrain. Historically it belonged to the Bushahr princely state and became a separate district in 1960; its distinctive Buddhist-Hindu culture and ancient trade routes remain important.

Economically, 2016–21: Apple/horticulture and hydropower were the two standout economic pillars, supplemented by dry fruits, tourism, livestock and government employment. Large hydro projects generated substantial investment and local compensation/development funds, but also created environmental and land-use tensions. Kinnaur's remote terrain constrained diversification even as high-value horticulture generated relatively strong agricultural incomes.

9. East Garo Hills, Meghalaya

Generally: East Garo Hills is a forested, hilly district in central Meghalaya, headquartered at Williamnagar. It became a full district in 1976 and was subsequently reduced in size when North Garo Hills was carved out in 2012. The climate is warm, humid and very monsoonal, with dense forests and substantial biodiversity.

Economically, 2016–21: The economy was predominantly agricultural, with horticulture, livestock, weaving, bamboo/cane work and small-scale processing providing livelihoods. Coal, limestone and clay were important resource endowments, but mining was constrained by environmental concerns and regulation. Limited industrialization and difficult terrain meant that better roads, agricultural value chains and food processing were key development opportunities.

Very rough economic comparison, 2016–21
District	Dominant economic character
Mewat/Nuh	Rain-fed agriculture + dairy; proximity to NCR but low industrialization
Pithoragarh	Agriculture + government/defence + migration + tourism
West Tripura	Urban services, government, trade + agriculture
Gonda	Large-scale agriculture + sugar industry
South Garo Hills	Subsistence/cash-crop agriculture + forestry/mining
Sivasagar	Oil & gas + tea + agriculture
Kargil	Agriculture + defence/public sector + tourism
Kinnaur	Apples/horticulture + hydropower
East Garo Hills	Agriculture/horticulture + small crafts + resource extraction

The biggest structural economic stories in 2016–21 were therefore: Sivasagar's oil/tea economy; Kinnaur's horticulture-hydropower model; Kargil's tourism/defence dependence; and the Garo Hills' struggle to move from subsistence agriculture/resource extraction toward higher-value agriculture and processing.
